## One scorer, many classifiers

Logistic regression outputs a probability; turning it into a label needs a threshold τ (lesson 3). Every τ gives a different classifier and a different confusion matrix. For scores where larger means “more likely positive”, predict positive when the score is at least τ. Lowering τ can only add positive predictions, so on a fixed dataset:

- TP and FP can only increase (or stay); FN and TN can only decrease.
- Recall therefore never decreases. Precision may go up or down — it depends on which observations are added.

Rather than pick one τ and report one matrix, we can look at the whole family at once.

## The ROC curve

The **receiver operating characteristic** curve plots the true-positive rate against the false-positive rate as the threshold sweeps from “nothing is positive” to “everything is positive”:

$$
\TPR = \frac{\TP}{\TP + \FN}\;\;(\text{of actual positives}), \qquad \FPR = \frac{\FP}{\FP + \TN}\;\;(\text{of actual negatives}).
$$

<Callout kind="warning">
FPR is *not* $1 - \Prec$. They share the numerator FP, but $1 - \Prec = \FP/(\TP + \FP)$ divides by the predicted positives, while FPR divides by the actual negatives.
</Callout>

<Steps>
Six observations, scores already sorted: $(0.95, 0.85, 0.75, 0.60, 0.40, 0.10)$ with labels $(1, 0, 1, 1, 0, 0)$ — three positives, three negatives. Each step of the threshold crosses one observation.

<Step title="Start above every score">
Nothing is predicted positive: $(\FPR, \TPR) = (0, 0)$.
</Step>

<Step title="Cross a positive ⇒ step up; cross a negative ⇒ step right">
Each positive crossed raises TPR by $1/3$; each negative raises FPR by $1/3$.
</Step>

<Step title="Trace the path">
| threshold | > 0.95 | 0.95 | 0.85 | 0.75 | 0.60 | 0.40 | 0.10 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FPR | 0 | 0 | 1/3 | 1/3 | 1/3 | 2/3 | 1 |
| TPR | 0 | 1/3 | 1/3 | 2/3 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
</Step>

<Step title="Area under the curve">
Only horizontal moves add area, each a strip of width 1/3 at the current height:

$$
\AUC = \tfrac13\cdot\tfrac13 + \tfrac23\cdot 1 = \tfrac79 \approx 0.778.
$$
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout kind="note" title="Tied scores">
Observations with equal scores must cross *together*, producing a diagonal segment. Do not give tied scores whichever order flatters the curve.
</Callout>

## Reading an ROC curve and its AUC

- The top-left corner combines high detection with few false alarms. A good model has AUC close to 1.
- AUC = 0.5 means *random ranking* — the diagonal is the reference line of a scorer that orders positives and negatives at random. It does not prove the algorithm literally guesses, and not every weak classifier sits exactly on the diagonal.
- AUC depends only on the **ordering** of the scores, so any strictly increasing transformation leaves the curve unchanged.

<Callout kind="intuition" title="Going deeper: AUC as a ranking probability">
AUC equals the probability that a randomly chosen positive is scored higher than a randomly chosen negative (ties counting ½). In the example the positive scores 0.95, 0.75, 0.60 beat 3, 2 and 2 of the negative scores 0.85, 0.40, 0.10: $7$ of $3 \times 3 = 9$ pairs, so AUC = $7/9$ again.
</Callout>

<Lab title="AUC as counting pairs" purpose="The unit square is cut into one cell per (positive, negative) pair. A cell is shaded when the positive scores higher. Drag the scores and compare the shaded fraction with the area under the ROC curve.">

<TryThis>
1. Worked example: 7 of the 9 cells are shaded, and the area under the curve is 7/9 ≈ 0.778 — the same number.
2. Drag the negative scored 0.85 below 0.60. The two pairs it was winning flip, all nine cells are shaded and AUC = 1.
3. Move every point by the same amount, or squeeze them together without changing their order: the grid and the AUC stay the same. AUC sees only the ranking.
4. Give a positive and a negative exactly the same score: their cell is half shaded. Ties count ½.
</TryThis>

AUC summarises performance *across* thresholds. It does not choose a threshold, guarantee well-calibrated probabilities, or tell you the accuracy at the operating point you will actually use. Choose τ for the task (on validation data), then report the metrics at that τ.

<Callout kind="key">
A scorer plus a threshold is a classifier; sweeping the threshold traces the ROC curve of $(\FPR, \TPR)$ pairs. </Callout>
