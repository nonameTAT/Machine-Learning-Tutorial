## The data

Fourteen days, three weather attributes and a label: did we play tennis? (The same days as Week 3’s Naive Bayes example, without Temperature.) Day only names the row; it is not a predictor — lesson 7 shows what goes wrong if you treat it as one.

Nine Yes and five No, so $H(S) = H\big(\tfrac{9}{14}\big) = 0.940286$ bits.

## Choose the root

Score every candidate with the same criterion, on the same 14 examples. Children are listed as (Yes, No).

<Table
          className="num"
          head={['Attribute', 'Children (Yes, No)', 'Expected H after', 'Gain']}
          rows={[
            ['Outlook', 'Sunny (2, 3) · Overcast (4, 0) · Rain (3, 2)', '0.693536', <strong>0.246750</strong>],
            ['Humidity', 'High (3, 4) · Normal (6, 1)', '0.788450', '0.151836'],
            ['Wind', 'Weak (6, 2) · Strong (3, 3)', '0.892159', '0.048127'],
          ]}
        />

<Steps>
<Step title="Child entropies for Outlook">
$$
H(2, 3) = H(3, 2) = 0.970951, \qquad H(4, 0) = 0.
$$
</Step>

<Step title="Weight by branch size">
$$
\tfrac{5}{14}(0.970951) + \tfrac{4}{14}(0) + \tfrac{5}{14}(0.970951) = 0.693536.
$$
</Step>

<Step title="Subtract from the parent">
$$
\operatorname{IG}(S, \text{Outlook}) = 0.940286 - 0.693536 = 0.246750.
$$
</Step>

<Step title="Compare and choose">
Outlook’s gain is the largest of the three: it becomes the root.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout kind="intuition" title="Why Outlook, in words">
Outlook helps because its answers change what we know about the label: Overcast becomes certain, and Sunny and Rain become smaller problems. It is not chosen because it has three branches, nor because every child is pure — two of them are not.
</Callout>

## Recurse inside each branch

Now each branch is its own problem, with its own counts.

- **Overcast** (D3, D7, D12, D13): four Yes. Pure — a leaf.
- **Sunny** (D1, D2, D8, D9, D11): 2 Yes, 3 No, $H = 0.970951$. Humidity gives High (0, 3) and Normal (2, 0) — both pure, so its gain is the full 0.970951. Wind gives (1, 2) and (1, 1): gain 0.019973. Choose Humidity.
- **Rain** (D4, D5, D6, D10, D14): 3 Yes, 2 No. Here it is the other way round: Wind gives Weak (3, 0) and Strong (0, 2), gain 0.970951; Humidity only 0.019973. Choose Wind.

## Read the tree as rules, and trace a prediction

| IF | THEN Play = |
| --- | --- |
| Outlook = Overcast | Yes |
| Outlook = Sunny ∧ Humidity = High | No |
| Outlook = Sunny ∧ Humidity = Normal | Yes |
| Outlook = Rain ∧ Wind = Weak | Yes |
| Outlook = Rain ∧ Wind = Strong | No |

The rules read off an unmodified tree are *mutually exclusive* (each input satisfies at most one) and *exhaustive* (each input satisfies at least one). To classify (Rain, High, Weak): the root sends it to Rain; the Rain node tests Wind, not Humidity; Weak leads to **Yes**. The humidity value was supplied but never consulted — the path decides which features matter.

<Callout kind="warning" title="Zero training error is a claim about 14 days">
The tree classifies all 14 training days correctly. That verifies the construction; it is not evidence of zero error on future days. Fitting the training data and generalising beyond it are different claims (lessons 10–12).
</Callout>
